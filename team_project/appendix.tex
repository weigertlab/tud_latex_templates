\latexTipsSection{General}

\begin{itemize}
\item Use present tense in general: ``For evaluation, we use the $F_1$ score \ldots''
``The advantage of the method is its lower memory use \ldots'' and ``We find that
performance improves with more training data.'' \etc
\item Use past tense for completed
experiments: ``We trained the model for 100 epochs.'', ``We collected 1000 samples from the dataset.'' \etc
\item Cite entries from \texttt{references.bib} by their key, \eg
\verb|\cite{he2016deep}| produces \cite{he2016deep}
\item Use the macros from \texttt{definitions.tex} for abbreviations such as
\verb|\eg|, \verb|\ie|, and \verb|\etc|, and for the number sets \verb|\R|,
\verb|\Z|, \verb|\N|, \verb|\Q|, and \verb|\C|. For example,
\verb|$x \in \R^n$| produces $x \in \R^n$
\item Add a \verb|\label| to sections, equations, figures, and tables and refer
to them with \verb|\ref|, \eg Equation~\ref{eq:f1-score}. Do not type reference
numbers manually
\item In general, refer to each figure and table in the text before it appears

\end{itemize}

\latexTipsSection{Equations}

Use \verb|$...$| for inline math, \eg \verb|$F_1$| produces $F_1$. Use the
\texttt{equation} environment for numbered equations:
\begin{equation}
  F_1 = \frac{2\,\mathrm{TP}}
  {2\,\mathrm{TP} + \mathrm{FP} + \mathrm{FN}},
  \label{eq:f1-score}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathrm{TP}\), \(\mathrm{FP}\), and \(\mathrm{FN}\) are the numbers of
true positives, false positives, and false negatives.

\latexTipsSection{Figures}

In general, use wide figures and place them at the top or bottom of a page.
\Eg Figure~\ref{fig:example-box-plot} uses the full text
width, while Figure~\ref{fig:data-visualizations} uses subfigures. Put captions
below figures and briefly explain all panels, symbols, units, \etc

\latexTipsSection{Tables}

Put table captions above the table. Use \texttt{booktabs}, omit vertical lines,
keep decimal places consistent, and bold the best value in each column, as in
Table~\ref{tab:example-results}.

\begin{table}[b]
  \centering
  \caption{\textbf{Example quantitative comparison.} We show accuracy, precision, and recall (higher is better) as well as error (lower is better) for three methods.}
  \label{tab:example-results}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
    \toprule
    Method & Accuracy $\uparrow$ & Precision $\uparrow$ & Recall $\uparrow$ & Error $\downarrow$ \\
    \midrule
    Method A & 0.81 & 0.80 & 0.78 & 0.23 \\
    Method B & 0.85 & \textbf{0.86} & \textbf{0.84} & 0.19 \\
    Method C & \textbf{0.89} & 0.84 & 0.82 & \textbf{0.15} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}
